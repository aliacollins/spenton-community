ENVELOPE BUDGETING

Give the money you have a clear plan.

Envelope budgeting means setting aside the money you already have for specific expenses and goals. Before buying something, check what is left in its category.

By SpentOn · Updated 14 September 2026 · 3 minute read

What is envelope budgeting?

In a cash-envelope system, you divide money between labelled envelopes. A digital budget uses categories instead. One might cover rent, another groceries and another an annual insurance bill.

Setting money aside changes your plan, not your bank balance. The money stays in the account where you hold it until you make a payment. SpentOn calls recorded budget cash that has not yet been set aside Available to plan.

Your bank still holds $1,000. Available to plan is $0 because you have set aside the full amount.

Check a category before spending

If you buy $40 of groceries, Groceries has $210 left and your bank balance falls to $960. Rent still has its $600 set aside. Looking only at the bank balance would hide that the rent money is already needed.

If a purchase costs more than a category has left, review it and decide whether to move category money from somewhere else. Moving category money changes your plan. It does not transfer money between bank accounts.

When priorities change, move category money to cover the expense and review what is left for your other plans.

Make your first plan

  1. Add your current account balances. Start with money you already hold. Scheduled future income is not spendable yet.
  2. Create a few useful categories. Begin with your bills and regular spending. Add more detail when it helps you make a decision.
  3. Use Plan your money. Set aside amounts for expenses that need paying before your next income arrives.
  4. Record purchases and review what is left. Add entries manually or review a CSV import. Adjust the plan when necessary.

Prepare for expenses that do not happen monthly

An annual bill is easier to plan for when you set aside money over time. A $1,200 renewal due in twelve months would need $100 each month if you are starting from zero. An earlier deadline or a missed contribution changes what is needed.

A savings goal suggests a contribution; setting the goal does not set aside the money for you. Record the actual amounts in your plan. Money left in a category carries forward, so you can build toward the expense over several months.

A few common questions

Is envelope budgeting the same as zero-based budgeting?

They share the idea of giving the money you hold a specific plan. A zero-based plan leaves no current money unallocated. That does not mean your bank balance should be zero.

Can I budget with irregular income?

Yes. Plan with income after you receive and record it, and prioritize the expenses that need paying next. A future payment you expect is not available cash today.

Does SpentOn connect to my bank?

No. You record balances and transactions manually or import a CSV and review it before saving. SpentOn does not move money between accounts.

Do I need to plan every category immediately?

No. Start with a few categories and the money you currently have. You can leave money available to plan and set it aside later when you decide what it needs to cover.

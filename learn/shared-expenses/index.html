SHARED EXPENSES

Split the bill. Keep your own budget.

When you pay for friends, the full bill leaves your account, but only your share is your personal spending. Keep the rest as money owed to you until it is repaid.

By SpentOn · Updated 14 September 2026 · 4 minute read

Your share is your spending

A shared expense divides a purchase between the people responsible for its cost. Your account records the full payment you made. Your personal spending records only your share. The remainder belongs under Friends owe you.

This keeps two useful numbers separate: what you spent on yourself and how much cash you currently have. Money someone owes you is not available cash, even when you expect them to repay you soon.

FICTIONAL EXAMPLE · US DOLLARS

A $120 dinner between three friends

You pay the bill, and everyone has an equal $40 share.

How the same dinner affects your records
RecordAmount
Payment from your account$120
Your personal spending$40
Friends owe you$80

The $80 is still outstanding. It is not money you can use to cover another bill.

Why your bank balance still matters

Suppose you start with $500 in the bank: $300 set aside for Bills, $120 for Dining and $80 available to plan. After paying the shared dinner, the bank holds $380. Dining has $80 left, Bills still has $300 set aside, and nothing is available to plan. The $80 you paid for friends is outstanding.

After both friends repay you and you record receiving the $80, the bank holds $460 and $80 becomes available to plan. Your personal spending for dinner remains $40.

Record a shared bill in SpentOn

  1. Record the purchase you paid for. Choose your account and the category for your share.
  2. Open People and split the purchase. Use equal shares or enter each person’s amount. You can name a participant before they join.
  3. Review the amounts. Keep the unpaid shares separate from your available cash. Send an invitation only when you choose to.
  4. Record money received after it arrives. Choose the receiving account, amount and date. Partial repayments are supported.

Friends using the same SpentOn server can choose a category in their own budget before accepting their share. An accepted share records their spending and sets aside available cash for repayment. Accepting a share does not send a payment.

SpentOn records repayments; it does not move money or confirm a payment with a bank. Make the payment through your usual bank or payment service, then record what happened.

A few common questions

Does a repayment count as new income?

Receiving repayment for a recorded shared expense reduces the amount friends owe you and updates the receiving account. It does not add new income or count the original spending again.

Can I use unequal shares or partial repayments?

Yes. Enter equal or custom shares when splitting the purchase. When money arrives, record the amount actually received, even if it is only part of what is owed.

Can I split any purchase?

Current sharing starts from a purchase in one category. Multi-category purchases cannot currently be shared. The bill and each participant’s budget must use the same currency; SpentOn does not convert currencies.

What happens if a friend has not joined?

You can keep a named participant and their amount in People before they join. You can also record money you received from them. That records your confirmation and does not imply that they accepted the share.
